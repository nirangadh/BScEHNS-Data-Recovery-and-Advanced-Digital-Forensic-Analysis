## Your role

You are an analyst in a **digital forensics unit**, a small team that investigates incidents for clients. A client company has found that confidential documents have left the company. The unit has been hired to find out what happened, how, and who was involved, and to report it in a way that would stand up in court.

Every session in this module answers part of that question. The techniques are always taught as a way to answer **what did the suspect do?** They are never taught on their own.

## The engagement

The suspect is an employee, called **Iaman Informant** in the evidence. He was in contact with someone outside the company, called **Spy Conspirator**. The evidence suggests he copied documents to removable media and then tried to hide his tracks with tools that wipe and clean data.

Our job is not to prove him guilty. It is to find what the evidence shows, test it, and report it honestly, including what it does not show.

## How the evidence reaches you

| Session | The evidence | Its place in the case |
|---|---|---|
| Before Day 1, Session 1 | A small test disk | The unit's check that you can copy and protect evidence correctly before you touch the real case. |
| Sessions 2, 4, 5, 14 | The suspect's computer and removable media | The heart of the engagement. |
| Session 3 | A disk from a closed earlier case | A practice case for recovering deleted files: the same technique on a smaller disk. |
| Session 6 | A memory capture | Taken from the suspect's computer while it was still running. You also practise the capture on your own machine. |
| Session 7 | Network traffic | Traffic from the wider investigation, plus a practice capture from the unit's training library. |
| Session 8 | Traffic from an infected computer | Found while the unit checked the network. |
| Sessions 9 and 10 | A suspicious program | Recovered from that computer and examined in a closed, safe lab. |
| Sessions 11 to 13 | A cloud storage account | Where the data was staged. You rebuild it yourself, to a given design, and investigate your own copy. This becomes your coursework. |
| Sessions 15 and 16 | The suspect's phone | A copy of its data, taken by the unit's mobile examiner. |
| Session 17 | A case review pack | Disk, network and cloud threads brought together before the final report. |

## A second client

Real units work on more than one case at a time. In Sessions 14 and 17 you also help a **second client**, a small online shop whose payment page has been tampered with. It is a short, separate case. It lets you practise web evidence and an incident-response decision without mixing it into the main story.

## Being honest about the evidence

The evidence comes from **public training datasets**, made by universities and standards bodies so that students and professionals can practise. The names, such as Iaman Informant, belong to those datasets. We weave them into one story, but we never pretend they are real people or a real case.

This honesty matters for a practical reason. Because the datasets are documented, you can check your findings against what is actually there. When a session says you should find something, there is a known answer.
