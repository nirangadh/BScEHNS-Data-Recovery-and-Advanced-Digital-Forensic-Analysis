What this shows: encryption hides the content of a connection, but not the handshake fields sent before the keys exist, and those fields depend on the TLS version.

WHAT YOU JUST SAW

You saw the same visit to files.example.com three ways, as an observer on the wire sees it. What is readable depends on the TLS version and on Encrypted Client Hello: the server name and the certificate in TLS 1.2, only the server name in TLS 1.3, and only the provider's shared name with ECH. The destination address, the sizes and the timing are always there. So an examiner can often say which site a device contacted and how much it sent, without ever reading a byte of the content, and must say which of these facts the capture really held.
